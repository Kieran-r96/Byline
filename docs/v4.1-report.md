# BYLINE V4.1: UX correction and visual design (22 Oct 2026)

Checkpoint before this work: git tag `pre-v4.1-ux` (the V4 build).

This pass followed two owner briefs, in order:
1. A targeted UX correction.
2. The creative direction mandate.

The Studio architecture, the Foundry provenance, the VAR safeguards, the storyline engine and the production functions were kept intact.

## 1. Design system: two themes, one identity

- **Two themes.**
  - **Night** is cinematic charcoal and navy. It is used for Studio and for every pitch, stage and monitor.
  - **Paper** is warm off-white. It is used for the Commentator's notes and the fan experience.
  - Any element can switch theme locally, so a pitch stays cinematic inside a paper page.
- **Colour meanings are fixed in both themes:**

  | Colour | Means |
  |---|---|
  | Flood | Live now, or the current selection |
  | Measured | Measured from tracking or events |
  | Verified | Verified by Foundry agents |
  | Hypo | Hypothetical, always dashed |
  | Against | What went wrong, corrected or withdrawn |
  | Club kits | Identity only |

- **Type.**
  - Instrument Sans for all reading text.
  - Archivo for display only: scores, shirt numbers, big moments and broadcast plates.
  - Condensed type is never used for ordinary reading.
  - There are no all-caps eyebrows in the reading UI; broadcast graphics keep their own conventions.
- **Contrast** is checked for every text and surface pair at 4.5:1 or better (axe: 0 serious or critical).

## 2. Commentator: calm and precise

The default screen has one focal column, beside the line-up.

- **The moment.** The newest official event or measured tactical discovery is the focal point.
  - Major events (goals, cards, substitutions, VAR) get a club-colour bar and larger type.
  - It can be marked used or dismissed. After six match minutes it settles into history and is replaced by "All caught up".
  - Card and VAR moments offer "Why that decision?".
  - A tactical moment shows the verified Foundry one-liner instead of the measurement paragraph; the measurements sit behind "Show the play".
- **Developing.** One measured tactical line and one pattern line, with a four-dot stage indicator (Watching, Building, Story, Confirmed).
- **Worth saying.** Two talking points, ranked by who has just been involved, each with "Mark used" and "Open file".
- **The line-up as shirts.**
  - Kit-coloured shirts on a floodlit pitch, with goals, cards and substitutions marked.
  - Clubs switch with one tap, and the shirts arrive in a short wave.
  - The bench sits alongside.
- **The player file** opens from a shirt:
  - identity, today's numbers and today's milestone;
  - the facts worth saying, with Pin to top and Mark used (with Undo);
  - the career by season, one tap away.
- **On demand:**
  - **Match so far:** key events, all developing items, all talking points and the match log.
  - **Stats.**
  - **Research:** search plus the club, head-to-head and venue files.
  - **The simulated match feed.**
  - These open in a side panel that keeps the moment in view.
- **The transport** is one slim row, with the scrubber riding on the match timeline.

## 3. Evidence navigation

- Show the play, See the evidence, Replay evidence and What if? open over the surface. They never navigate away.
- **Back to match** is the same unmistakable button everywhere. Escape closes only the top layer.
- The match pauses while you study a moment. Back to match returns the playhead to exactly where it was, at the same speed, playing only if it was playing.
- The 3D explanation no longer plays by itself: Next, Back, Pause/Play, Replay the real play and the speed controls are under the viewer's hands.

## 4. Fan: quick and welcoming

- The scoreboard puts the two clubs' colours side by side, with large score numbers.
- **Who are you with today?** Two club-colour buttons, then **Pick your player** from the starting XI as shirts.
- **What would you do?** leads the feed:
  1. The real frozen frame appears, with the real pass hidden.
  2. Three shirt choices.
  3. The reveal: what happened, how open each option was in plain words, and the verified Foundry read.
  4. "Show me why" opens the guided 3D explanation.
- **Feed:** goal moments in club colour, cards and substitutions as quiet lines, and "Did you know?" limited to three in the main feed with the number as the picture.
- **Stats** wait behind "Match stats". Classic stays minimal.

## 5. Studio: broadcast quality (Live production and Analysis desk kept)

- **Seven graphic families in a BYLINE house style:**
  - player spotlight (the shirt in kit colours, number, name, minute, goals in the match);
  - tactical breakthrough (a mini pitch from the measured frame);
  - record and milestone (the number is the hero);
  - substitution and shape;
  - match comparison (bars from the centre);
  - refereeing decision (the law, the call, the corrected state);
  - story lower third, marked "Verified analysis" only when Foundry verified it.
- **Motion:** clip-path wipes and masked, staggered text, 460 to 700 ms, with no bounce. Under reduced motion graphics simply fade.
- **Template-driven workflow** (simulated, local only):
  - A broadcaster's template is data: its tokens, layout, its own field names and its family names. "Sample partner template: Northgate Sports (fictional)" sits beside the BYLINE house style.
  - One fill step populates both the drawn graphic and the inspector's "Filled from verified data" table: template field, value, source (official event, measured, Foundry verified, fictional history, team sheet or counted) and evidence id. The producer checks exactly what will air.
  - Labelled "Simulated output · local demo · no broadcast integration".
- **The animated tactical breakdown is a graphics asset.** When a tactical graphic is previewed or on air, its guided 3D clip loops under the telestration plate on the monitor. There are never more than two WebGL contexts, and both are released while a modal is open.
- **The monitors:**
  - A multiviewer pair with a tally lamp, a lit programme border on air, and a score and clock bug with a VAR tab.
  - A "SIMULATED OUTPUT" mark and title-safe and action-safe guides.
  - Preview slates for held, withdrawn and done graphics.
- **The rundown:** distinct states for on air, scheduled, VAR padlock, withdrawn and producer request, with 16:9 thumbnails.
- **Analysis desk:** a timeline library, a compact header, played-against-hypothetical as a bar chart (hypothetical bars dashed lilac), and a numbered package rundown showing each item's live production state.
- **Safeguards kept:** a human approves every graphic; a VAR hold cannot be overridden; corrections withdraw graphics; scheduling (now, next stoppage, 30 s); producer proposals.

## 6. The tactical stage: one idea per step, comfortable pace, simulated continuation

- **The steps.** A What If? moment now has seven, each with one idea:
  1. The decision.
  2. Their lines.
  3. What happened (only the real pass).
  4. Measured options (the comparison).
  5. What if? (the hypothetical only).
  6. Then? (simulated).
  7. Takeaway.

  Tests enforce at most three arrows a step, and the real pass never appears alongside a hypothetical or simulated layer.
- **Pace.** Nothing advances by itself. A large Next button names the next step; Back sits beside it. Optional Auto-play holds each step at least 7 s, and layers appear one at a time.
- **Simulated continuation (new backend geometry).**
  - From each measured alternative's frozen position, with everyone else held still, BYLINE computes up to two next passes, a shooting window and a crossing option, each only when the geometry supports it. The window needs at most 25 m to goal, at least 12° of goal mouth and at most one defender in the triangle. The cross needs a wide channel in the final third with a team-mate in the box.
  - The stage draws it paler and dotted, labelled "Simulated continuation" and "Simulated · not what happened · no outcome implied". At 7' it is one contested pass from Haddad-Rhys to Vantreight (lane 7.2 m, space 2.7 m).
  - No probability, no "would", no outcome. The agents' inputs are unchanged, so the recorded Foundry run still matches its key.
- **Visuals:**
  - Upright shirt markers with legible numbers.
  - "Name · on the ball" for the carrier.
  - Amber reserved for AI-verified text.
  - Floodlit grass with blade detail, stripes and haze, with player positions unchanged.
- **The verified Foundry line** appears on the takeaway step. A moment withdrawn after a correction is greyed and struck through.
