# Architecture decision records (succinct)

Key implementation decisions and the reasons for them.

1. **Python 3.12 runtime.** Microsoft Agent Framework and Azure SDK wheels are best supported on 3.12. The machine also has 3.14, but it is not used.
2. **Foundry access path.** The official OpenAI SDK `AsyncOpenAI(base_url=<Foundry>/openai/v1, api_key=<Entra bearer-token provider>)` is wrapped in Agent Framework's `OpenAIChatCompletionClient`. Auth is `DefaultAzureCredential` with scope `https://ai.azure.com/.default`, and the tenant can be pinned. This matches the owner's Foundry sample. No keys are stored.
3. **Agent orchestration.** The workflow is an Agent Framework `WorkflowBuilder` graph. Its Executors each wrap a separate `Agent` with its own instructions, tools and structured output type. A conditional edge gives one verifier → analyst revision loop. A deterministic validator is mandatory after the verifier.
4. **Hard model-call ledger.** A persistent, cross-process cap (`BYLINE_MODEL_CALL_CAP`) is charged before every real request. It adds to the per-session, per-day, per-case-token and concurrency limits and the circuit breaker. The status endpoint never spends a call.
5. **Cache-only mode and recorded runs.** `BYLINE_AI_CACHE_ONLY=true` serves recorded real Foundry results, which are committed under `apps/api/recorded/insights` with traces, and never starts new calls. These are cache hits of genuine inference, flagged `from_cache` with original response ids. They are never presented as fresh runs.
6. **Contract extensions** (backward compatible):
   - Shot `outcome` gains `entered_goal`, used for an indirect free kick entering the goal untouched.
   - The `injury.stoppage` payload may carry `player_id`.
   - `offside.offence_confirmed` may carry `centre_coordinate_margin_m`.
7. **Half-time compression.** The replay timeline jumps 5 s at half-time. Displayed clocks are computed per period, so they are unaffected.
8. **Retraction is a per-session view.** Insights and graphics are append-only. A session sees a retraction (version+1, reason) only once its own playhead reaches the correcting official event.
9. **Task runner.** `tools/run.py` (Python) replaces `make`, which is not available on a stock Windows machine. The `Makefile` delegates to it.
10. **Simulated continuations on the What If? payload (additive).** `whatif` gains `continuations` and `continuation_rules`. For each measured alternative, its receiver is assumed on the ball at their frozen position with everyone else still, and the same frame is measured again: next passes (the existing options geometry), a shooting window (≤ 25 m, goal mouth ≥ 12°, ≤ 1 outfield opponent in the ball-to-posts triangle) and a crossing option (wide channel in the final third, an onside team-mate in the box). Every item is labelled "Simulated continuation … Not what happened … no outcome is implied", carries only measurements, and is drawn in its own style (lilac dotted) after the hypothetical step. The keys sit outside `labels` and `rules`, so the agents' case and the recorded Foundry run are unchanged (tested). Alternatives' `bypassed_ids` now list only the opponents between the ball and the receiver, matching `opponents_bypassed`.
   - **Illustrative continuation (moving players), added in the final pass.** `whatif` also gains `illustrative` (or `null`) and `illustrative_rules`. These hold one sketched sequence for the best alternative only, built from the frozen frame and published constants. There is no look-ahead, no model and no probability.
     - **When it is drawn:** only when the alternative receiver stands in a crossing position (wide channel, final third) and an onside team-mate stands in the central channel. Otherwise it is `null` and the stage keeps the geometry-only continuation above.
     - **Speeds and timing:** the pass travels at 16 m/s. The receiver takes 1 s to control. Every mover runs at 7 m/s, attackers and defenders alike, so the sketch does not favour either side. Defenders react 0.3 s after each ball. The ball in travels at 18 m/s.
     - **The run:** the most advanced central team-mate runs towards the penalty spot. The offside line is held where it stood, and the run stops 0.5 m short of it.
     - **Defender response:** the receiver's nearest marker closes to 1.5 m goal-side. The runner's nearest opponent tracks him to 1 m goal-side. Everyone else stays still.
     - **Contests:** a *block* is a defender within 1 m of the ball's first 3 m (the ball is assumed in the air after that). A *possible interception* is any opponent who can reach the delivery point before the ball.
     - **The 7' moment (T-option-NBA-0180):** the switch to Haddad-Rhys (37.3 m). Vantreight runs 10.5 m and is held onside at 85.7 m, outside the box. Tremaine closes 7.8 m goal-side, and Ndiaye-Shaw tracks 14.2 m. The ball in (30.4 m) could be blocked by Tremaine, and Ndiaye-Shaw could reach the delivery point first.
     - **How it is shown:** on screen it reads "Illustrative · not what happened · no outcome implied". It is drawn as translucent "ghost" shirts with dotted trails, distinct from the real play (solid, kit and coral) and from the measured hypothetical (lilac dashed).
     - **Key safety:** the keys sit outside `labels` and `rules`, so the recorded Foundry run's key is unchanged (tested; `GET …/T-option-NBA-0180/insight` still returns the verified run).
